# Child Interaction Research: Primary Sources

Accessed 2026-11-01. Audience: children aged 7-10 and parents. This supports interaction exploration, not a change to accounting/release requirements or proof of child validation. Translation does not constitute a fresh source review.

## Conclusion and evidence limits

There is no evidence here that steering a squirrel to a building before opening a business panel is more popular with the target children than direct app controls. Story, character, and feedback can remain, but test town appeal, task discovery, and understanding of when money arrives separately.

Eight products were checked using first-party feature material, alongside original NN/g usability research. Downloads, ratings, curated testimonials, vendor claims, and younger-child examples do not establish preferences for ages 7-10. Proposed Pennywood designs remain hypotheses.

Distinguish product facts (official features), vendor claims (motivation, habit formation, non-addiction), original usability observations (age/interaction risks), and our design inferences. Sources were official page bodies and developer descriptions returned by Apple's Lookup API. Apps were not installed, children were not tested, and inaccessible/script-only pages were not treated as evidence. Store content ratings are not instructional target ages.

## Original usability research

[Children's UX: Usability Issues in Designing for Young People](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/childrens-websites-usability-issues/), Katie Sherwin and Jakob Nielsen, 2019-01-13, summarizes three rounds: 55 children aged 6-11 in 2001, 35 aged 3-12 in 2010, and 35 aged 3-12 in 2018 (125 total). The last round covered 39 websites and 36 apps, roughly half in the US and half in Beijing. This is historical research, not a 2026 survey of target families.

Observed: children skip long instructions, expect pictures/characters to respond, and may leave after several failures. Separate ages 3-5, 6-8, and 9-12; even a year or two can change perceived suitability. Consistent conventional UI still helps; device familiarity does not imply complex-system understanding. For Pennywood, observe 7-8 and 9-10 separately, expose important actions, and avoid long dialogue as the only teaching mechanism.

[Design for Kids Based on Their Stage of Physical Development](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/children-ux-physical-development/), Feifei Liu, 2018-07-08, draws on observations of ages 3-12 in the US and China. Do not count it as an independent preference sample from the study above. It recommends large obvious targets, taps, swipes, and forgiving touch dragging. Precision placement and narrow targets cause failures: two seven-year-olds abandoned a roughly 5 mm close target. Offer tapping as an alternative to dragging and reduce unnecessary navigation. A Pennywood coin animation must never require precise insertion to post money; walking and camera navigation are costs to test separately.

## Product mechanisms

### NatWest Rooster Money

[Chores](https://roostermoney.com/feature/chore-app-rooster-money/) supports parent one-off/recurring tasks and child completion submission for approval: tap a task, Done, optional photo, submit. The [virtual tracker](https://roostermoney.com/feature/pocket-money-allowance-tracker/) includes pots, goals, saving/spending history, automatic allowances, manual additions, and parent-set interest. [Stars](https://roostermoney.com/feature/kids-reward-chart-app/) is a distinct reward-chart mode, including a target pot.

Tracker/Stars pages say 3+; card/subscription information gives ages 6-17, parents 18+, UK residency. Card eligibility is not a validated preference age for every mode. Borrow direct task approval, visible funds/goals, and different parent/child complexity. Do not copy automatic allowance as earned labor, turn stars into a second redeemable cash economy, or require photos as proof.

### Greenlight

[Chores and allowance](https://greenlight.com/chores-and-allowance-app-for-kids) supports task pay, flexible schedules, goals, completion records, and extra-task/reminder/review mechanisms. [Level Up](https://greenlight.com/level-up-financial-literacy-game) uses short videos, mini-games, and questions for kids/teens. No checked page supplied a controlled 7-10 interaction-preference study; scale, testimonials, and motivational wording are marketing evidence only.

Borrow one clear action per card and brief illustrated financial explanations. Courses, streaks, and taps must not credit real Pennywood money. Do not import bank cards, actual investing, automatic payments, commercial rates, or streak conditions on already-earned pay.

### Toca Boca World

The [official page](https://www.tocaboca.com/toca-boca-world) describes character creation, house design, exploration, and self-expression, not financial forms. No precise 7-10 preference study was verified; vendor player counts do not prove appeal. Actual gestures were not tested.

A front-facing room with directly tappable envelopes, wallet, and wish frame may preserve ownership without walking/camera controls. That is our inference, not a Toca finance feature. Weekly gifts/secrets must not pay real cash, decorations must not force principal spending, and objects must not become undiscoverable hidden buttons.

### Pok Pok

The [home page](https://playpokpok.com/) presents press/pinch/swipe/toggle toys, Drawing, House, Town, and World Puzzle. The [FAQ](https://playpokpok.com/faqs/) describes open play without right/wrong or win/lose, with slow animation and gentle sound. The home page says ages 2-8; FAQ emphasizes 2-7. Both skew younger and do not establish appeal for 9-10. Non-addiction and low-overstimulation are vendor design claims, not experimentally confirmed effects here.

Borrow forgiving, immediate responses for non-accounting objects and optional exploration that can end freely. Money confirmation still needs clear amounts/consequences. Do not hide financial actions inside toy behavior or assume a preschool style fits all targets.

### Pokemon Smile

The [official page](https://smile.pokemon.com/en-us/) describes camera-assisted brushing, rescuing characters, hats/photo decoration, achievements, one-to-three-minute timers, and reminders. No precise target-age comparison or behavior-improvement trial was verified. Motivational marketing is not retention evidence.

Borrow a bounded ritual after real effort: a stamp, sticker, or short story after parent confirmation. This does not justify camera/AR collection. Self-report or a photo cannot automatically qualify work or credit income. Keep submission, checking, and payment separate.

### Duolingo ABC

The developer's [store entry](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/learn-to-read-duolingo-abc/id1440502568), read through the [US Lookup API](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1440502568&country=us), describes short lessons, interactive stories, tracing/dragging, word-highlighted narration, and feedback. Preschool-to-second-grade positioning does not cover all ages 7-10; 4+ content rating is not difficulty guidance. The official ABC site returned a script prompt and was not used for detailed claims.

Borrow finite story panels and optional narration, not classroom quizzes replacing real work. Passing lessons or keeping streaks cannot add real money. Test tone and characters separately with 9-10-year-olds.

### Khan Academy Kids

The developer's [entry](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/khan-academy-kids/id1378467217), read through the [US Lookup API](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1378467217&country=us), describes characters associated with reading/writing/numeracy, interactive books, Read To Me, learning activities, and parent/teacher tools. Ages 2-8/preschool-to-second-grade do not establish 9-10 preferences. Awards, scale, and curated reviews are not usability evidence. The original site was access-blocked.

Borrow companion guidance, visual choice, and narration without requiring character movement. Avoid a large course library/recommendation feed that shifts the main path away from real family agreements and toward screen time.

### Finch

The [official introduction](https://finchcare.com/about-finch) connects small goals/self-care to energy, pet adventures/stories, outfits, room decoration, and journaling. It is a cross-category reference, not a verified 7-10 product. The [store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/finch-self-care-pet/id1528595748) 4+ content rating does not establish child suitability. Terms/privacy links returned script shells; contractual age limits could not be confirmed, so neither 13+ nor child suitability is asserted.

Borrow emotional squirrel responses and brief stories without an entire game map. Do not add hunger, illness, missed-day penalties, or convert self-reported checkmarks into actual income. A separate pet economy is unnecessary in the first comparison.

## Five exploration designs

These are distinct Pennywood hypotheses, not reskinned versions of one form.

| Design | Child action | Mechanism reference | Hypothesis | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A: One thing today | One current task, large accept/submit action | Rooster, Greenlight, NN/g reduced navigation | Direct next steps improve understanding and return intent | May feel like assigned homework; test agency |
| B: Pocket-money desk | Tap wallet, pots, wish; choose amount and confirm; drag optional | Rooster pots/history, forgiving gestures | Visible partitions explain money better than account terms | Decorative coins can mislead amounts/distract |
| C: My little room | Tap envelope/wallet/frame directly | Toca expression, Pok Pok exploration | Ownership survives without walking/camera cost | Hidden affordances or a childish tone |
| D: Wish puzzle | Progress/gap first, tap available work | Rooster goals; Pok Pok puzzle as visual inspiration only | Visible distance helps children understand accumulation | Pressure to overwork; projected returns shown as owned |
| E: Comic task journal | One image/action per finite panel | Duolingo ABC short stories; Khan narration/characters | Story explains work -> checking -> payment | Slows practiced users; mandatory page rituals |

A is the simplest app control, B tests money representation, C tests spatial ownership, D tests goal motivation, and E tests sequence comprehension. Avoid mixing all five initially. Use the same isolated demo: CNY 32 cash, an CNY 8 real task, still 32 after submission/checking, 40 only after actual payment confirmation. Never import these demo funds into a real family.

## Shared accounting boundaries

From [accounting rules](../02-accounting-rules.md), not competitors: acceptance/submission/checking/exploration do not credit money; actual payment does, once. Stickers are not redeemable cash. Separate cash, holdings, pending withdrawals, debt, and projections. Progress must be traceable, avoid double-counting transferred principal, and exclude projections. Required deposits/withdrawals/repayments work by tap, with feedback after saving. Children remain isolated and parent actions explicitly name the child. Prototype approval controls demonstrate sequence, not production authorization.

## Proposed child validation

This is an unexecuted small-sample exploration, not a statistical-significance claim.

1. With parental consent, recruit 4-6 children aged 7-8 and 4-6 aged 9-10. Parents may attend but should not answer the first attempt. Report groups separately.
2. Give each child two or three designs in counterbalanced order; five repeats cause transfer/fatigue. Narrow to two before testing differences.
3. Match demo amounts, tasks, and visual fidelity. Observe finding, accepting, submitting, waiting, and checking cash after parent payment. Record first tap, prompts, checking/payment confusion, and back navigation.
4. Ask current money, who must do what next, and preferred next-use design/why. Do not ask only whether it is fun or imply games are superior.
5. Return after one or two days with a different task. Novelty is not repeated-use intent.
6. Separate task success, accounting understanding, and preference. Small samples discover issues, not universal age-group conclusions.
